          Deflection settings

          Laser angle: read it on the meter (level or indirect mode) with the meter in place, then switch back to single distance mode; a negative angle (pointing down) sets Laser to Down.           Guide

          Planum connects your phone to a Bosch laser meter over Bluetooth. Its main job: take a photo and write the meter's distance and angle into the photo file, so you can later measure from pixels in Markup or Digital Crack Gauge. It also logs every reading, scans surface profiles with the meter on a slider, records walking profiles with a two-foot carriage (dipstick), and tracks how far a structure deflects over time. Everything stays on your phone. Tap a topic to open it.

          Getting started
          • Use Chrome on an Android phone.
          • Switch the meter on and turn its Bluetooth on.
          • Tap Connect and pick the meter in the list. The dot turns green and the meter's model shows at the top.
          • Pick a tab: Spot for single readings and photos, Profile for dipstick walks, Scan for slider scans, Deflection for movement of one spot over time. The big button follows the tab.
          • In Spot, tap Measure: the app switches the laser on and takes the reading.
          • If the link drops, the app reconnects by itself.
          Photos with measurementsmain use
          • In Spot, Photo is on when the app opens (tap it to switch photos off); tap Measure. Each photo carries its reading inside the file: distance, angle and tilt, meter mode and model, time, and GPS if turned on.
          • Later, open the photo in Markup or Digital Crack Gauge and measure from pixels: the recorded distance sets the scale.
          • For a true scale: hold the phone square to the surface, and set Distance offset Z (Spot settings, under the readings list) so the distance counts from the camera, not the meter.
          • Full quality: your phone's camera app opens. Take the photo and confirm it. Best picture; the reading is stored as XMP.
          • In browser: the app takes the photo from its live view straight after the reading. Faster; the reading is stored as EXIF.
          • Photos are taken in Spot only: Profile and Scan switch the camera off, and Spot turns it back on as you left it.
          • Photos are saved to the phone's Downloads, named with the file prefix, reading number and time. On Windows, the reading summary shows in the file's Properties, Details tab.
          Spot readings
          • Choose the mode on the meter: distance, continuous, angle, level, indirect height or indirect length. The app reads them all.
          • Continuous readings show min and max in the list; indirect readings show the vertical (V) and horizontal (H) parts.
          • Level mode shows the angle along the beam and the side tilt.
          • Readings taken with the meter's own button are logged too.
          • The Spot tab lists your readings: Export CSV or Clear log.
          Profile (dipstick)new, unvalidated
          • Measures a floor profile step by step. Fix the meter flat on a carriage with two feet (e.g. 300 mm apart) and set the meter to level mode.
          • Open the Profile tab, check the step length and walk style (Profile settings), put the carriage on the start mark and tap the big Start profile button; Stop ends it.
          • The app reads the angle about 5 times a second. Move the carriage to the next step, let go and hold still for about 1–2 s: when the readings agree (3 by default), it records the step, beeps and buzzes.
          • Pivot walk: swing the carriage 180° over the front foot each step. The app flips the angle's sign every other step. Set which way the laser end points on the first step.
          • Zero check: do it first. In a pivot walk the carriage swaps ends every step, so any zero error (meter zero plus a foot that is a little longer) shifts every other foot mark by spacing × error: 0.1° is about 0.5 mm on a 300 mm carriage. Closure, the averaged pair and Compare can't show it, and it lowers floor flatness numbers a lot.
          • How: mark two spots for the feet. Tap Calibrate zero, put the carriage on the marks and hold still: it records a placement. Lift it, put it back on the same marks, hold still; repeat (5 by default). Then it beeps and asks you to turn the carriage 180° onto the same marks and do the same again. It works out the zero error (z) and how sure it is, and subtracts it from every reading of the profiles that follow.
          • Do another zero check at the end of the survey: the app shows the drift, and the profiles in between use the mean of the two checks. The viewer lets you switch the correction off, or apply the latest check to an older profile. The meter's own angles stay unchanged in the CSV; corrected columns sit beside them.
          • If the zero is large (over about 0.05°), adjust a foot until a placement and its 180° turn read the same size with opposite sign, lock the foot, and check again.
          • Stance: stand to the side at mid-length, about 1 m away, hands off while it records, same place each step. On timber or other suspended floors your weight tilts the carriage a lot; slab-on-ground concrete was not affected in tests.
          • Walk back over the same marks: tap Return walk where the walk ended, carriage as it is (don't turn it round). The app sets the sign itself (odd or even step count) and shows when you're back at the start. Stop, and it shows the closure, the bias per step, the scatter between the walks and the averaged profile (which cancels most handling bias). A pair CSV is saved too.
          • The pair also compares odd and even steps. If their differences don't match (shown as an alternating pattern), one foot is probably shifting depending on the walking direction: check the feet are tight and locked.
          • Compare two profiles (tap Compare on each): B − A foot by foot, e.g. the same marks with and without a bump, or two repeat walks. Returns are turned round to match; tick Use averaged pairs to compare there-and-back averages.
          • Keep the carriage upright: side tilt over 2° gives a low beep and a flag (0.4° error per 60° of tilt, about 0.04 mm per step at 1°). If the carriage tips onto its side the meter switches to angle mode: the profile pauses and carries on once it's back in level mode.
          • Can't settle (vibration)? After 10 s a Record step button appears to save it by hand. Wrong step? Undo step, then hold still to measure again.
          • Each profile is saved as a CSV (elevation of every foot position) plus a raw file with every reading. Keep the app in front with the screen on.
          • Field tests so far (4 Oct): a walk there and back closes to 0.2–3 mm over 12–16 m; averaged walks repeat to about 0.1 mm per step; a 6.3 mm bump read within 0.1 mm; the carriage re-seats to 0.004–0.008°; a zero error of −0.12° was found and adjusted to −0.016°. Not yet checked against a level and staff. Floor flatness numbers (F-numbers) are not calculated.
          Scan (slider)
          • For surface profiles. Fix the meter square to the surface (pointing down, up or sideways) on a slider or carriage moving at a steady speed.
          • Set the meter to single distance mode. Open the Scan tab, enter the speed in mm/s in Scan settings if you know it, then tap the big Start scan button; Stop ends it. Don't press the meter's buttons during a scan.
          • GLM 50-27 CG: about 2.5 readings per second, not stored on the meter. GLM 100-25 C: about 1.2 per second using automatic button presses; readings are also stored in the meter's memory.
          • Keep the app in front with the screen on: Chrome slows down in the background.
          • Each scan is saved as a CSV file (automatic by default). View draws the profile; touch the chart to read values.
          • Rail correction removes the sag of one particular slider (the author's). Leave it off for any other setup.
          Deflectionnew, unvalidated
          • Tracks how much a beam, slab or other structure moves at one spot over time, e.g. under a test load or through the day. The meter stays off the structure (tripod, pier, bank) with the laser on one spot.
          • Set up: fix the meter rigidly (a tripod on concrete beats one on soil), aim at the spot and set single distance mode. Read the laser angle on the meter (level or indirect mode) without moving it, then switch back to single distance mode.
          • In Deflection settings enter the angle, whether the laser points down at a top face or up at a soffit, and the surface slope along the laser (0 = flat; positive = rising away from you). The line under the help shows how much the distance changes per mm of deflection and how far the spot slides.
          • Tap Start and keep your hands off: the meter warms up (60 s by default; its reading drifts by about 0.5 mm at first), then the app sets the zero over 10 s and beeps. From then on the big number is the deflection (median of the last 5 s, so someone walking through the beam doesn't count), positive = down.
          • Tap Load on and Load off when a load goes on and comes off, Mark for anything else, Zero now to start again from the current position. The chart shows each one.
          • At the end: the peak for each load, what is left after unloading, and the drift. Remove drift takes a straight line through the unloaded readings out of the chart; the CSV keeps both.
          • The spot slides along the surface as it moves. On a flat face this is fully allowed for (bench test 7 Oct: within 0.5%, slope correction needed, about 1%). A bump or joint under the moving spot goes straight into the result: aim at a smooth patch, or fix a small plate square to the beam (Target: Plate).
          • Limits: about 2.5 readings a second (1.2 on the 100-25 C), so slow movement only, not vibration. On a long, shallow shot the mount matters most: a tripod tilting by 0.01° looks like about 3.5 mm at 20 m. Results are indicative, not a certified load-test record.
          • Long runs: choose 5 s or 30 s in Store (one median per period), keep Planum in front with the screen on, and use a power bank. Each run is saved as a CSV (automatic by default).
          Readings and files
          • The Spot tab lists what you've measured. They are stored in this phone's browser only (up to 3000).
          • Export CSV saves them as a file. Clear log deletes them from the phone; exported files and photos are not affected.
          • Scans, profiles and deflection runs are kept separately (last 20 each): Export or Delete each one.
          • Clearing Chrome's site data deletes everything stored here, so export regularly.
          Settings
          • Each tab has its own settings under its list: Spot settings, Profile settings, Scan settings, Deflection settings.
          • Spot settings: Distance offset Z moves readings to another reference point, such as the phone camera (the reading without offset is saved too); GPS location, saved with each reading; photograph meter-button readings (In browser only).
          • App settings (gear button at the bottom, next to i): the file name prefix for all saved files, and the Bluetooth frame log, a diagnostic record of the meter traffic for troubleshooting.
          • Camera blocked? Tap the icon left of the address › Permissions › Camera › Allow, then tap Photo.
          What it supports
          • GLM 50-27 CGtested all features.
          • GLM 100-25 Ctested all features; scans are slower.
          • Other Bosch GLM “C” models with Bluetooth, e.g. GLM 50-27 C, 150-27 C, 120 C, 150 Clikely, untested Please report how they go.
          • Chrome on Android only. Not iPhone or Firefox: they have no Web Bluetooth.
          • Area, volume, double indirect and trapezoid results are shown on the meter but not logged yet.

          Tips and limits
          • Bosch's accuracy spec is ±1.5 mm (typical). Repeat readings scatter by about 0.1 mm at short range, more at long range or on dark, shiny or angled surfaces.
          • Check your setup against a known length before relying on results.
          • Unofficial: the app uses an undocumented meter protocol and could stop working after a meter firmware update.
          • Something wrong? Use Report a bug in About (the i button at the bottom).
